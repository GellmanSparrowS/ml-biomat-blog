Biomacromolecules Group   /   Advisor: Prof. Shengjie Ling

Rational design of biomaterial systems and structures across scales

Yunhao Yang is a Ph.D. student in the Department of Macromolecular Science at Fudan University and previously worked as an engineer at its Zhangjiang Institute. His research combines physics-informed artificial intelligence and multiscale simulation for the rational design of biomaterial systems and structures across scales. He has published more than ten papers in journals including Nature Communications. His honors include a runner-up finish in the AI for Research Track of the Inaugural World AI Open Source Competition, Second Prize in the Achievement Category of the China Invention Association's Invention and Entrepreneurship Award (third contributor), and the Ruiqing Scholarship.

Nature Communications · 2026

Designing manufacturable fibrous networks

Continuous fiber paths and manufacturing constraints are incorporated into topology design. Finite element analysis, physics-inspired graph neural networks, and reinforcement learning connect digital structures to 3D-printed materials.

A manufacturability-informed topology framework for AI-guided design of fibrous network materials

Yang Y., Ren J., Cao L. et al. · 17, 9237

npj Flexible Electronics · 2026

Structural optimization of silk fibroin ionic conductors

Zigzag silk fibroin ionic conductors connect geometric parameterization, performance prediction, and reinforcement learning with structural optimization for spatially resolved wearable motion detection.

Machine Learning–Driven Structural Optimization of Zigzag Silk Fibroin Ionic Conductors for Spatially Resolved Wearable Motion Detection

Yang Y., Sun S., Xu C. et al.

Nano Letters · 2026

Learning the deformation of disordered fibrous networks

This collaborative work incorporates network connectivity and physical information into graph neural networks to predict nonuniform deformation and stress–strain responses in disordered fibrous materials.

Physics-informed graph neural networks for predicting deformation in disordered fibrous materials

Yang S.#, Yang Y.#, Huang C. et al. · 26(1), 205–213

Advanced Science · 2025

Lightweight, high-strength 3D fibrous networks

Tensile simulation and deep reinforcement learning are combined for biomimetic three-dimensional disordered fibrous networks, exploring structural changes that bring lightweight design and mechanical performance together.

Optimizing biomimetic 3D disordered fibrous network structures for lightweight, high-strength materials via deep reinforcement learning
